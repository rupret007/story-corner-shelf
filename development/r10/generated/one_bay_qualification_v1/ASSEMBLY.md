# R10 one-bay tabletop assembly

> **Qualification article only — rated load 0 kg / 0 lb.** This is one actual
> Lincoln-log shelf cell, not a scale model, but it is not a full-wall release.
> Assemble it only on a flat tabletop. Do not drill the closet wall, attach any
> part to a wall, place stored load on it, or infer a load rating from a good
> fit. Nothing in this document grants permission to start a printer.

## What this assembly proves

The core assembly checks the real R10 interfaces for one 254 mm planning bay:

- two printed supports;
- two regular cassette halves;
- three independent splice logs at the rear, center, and front;
- three independent log retainers with integrated flush access caps; and
- two bay-local support retainers with positive reversible walk-out stops.

These are the same structural article types intended for the first shelf. A
passing tabletop assembly qualifies only their fit, handling, and repeatable
dry assembly. It does not qualify the six-bay wall, wall connection, PETG
creep, proof load, cable clearance in the room, or any allowable load.

The S0 cable-bookend set is a separate four-article furniture-interface gate.
Its fused receiver, blanks, and comb/hook have zero structural or shelf-load
credit. Do not add the S0 bookend as a third support in the one-bay assembly.

The 16-article qualification bundle and the eventual 70-article first-wall
count answer different questions. The bundle contains 12 core one-bay articles
plus four S0 cable-gate articles. The complete 61.25 in first-wall topology is
seven supports (with the fused S0 bookend occupying one of those seven support
positions), 12 cassette halves, 18 splice logs, 18 flush-capped log retainers,
12 bay-local support retainers, two flush blanks, and one comb/hook: exactly
**70 printed articles**. The ordinary left support and fused S0 bookend are
both present in the qualification bundle so each interface can be isolated;
they are not both installed at S0 in the eventual wall.

## Before touching the parts

Prepare a clean, padded, flat table, digital calipers, a verified square, a
straightedge, feeler gauges, a soft brush, the print/inspection record from
`PRINT_FIRST.md`, and a camera. No adhesive, lubricant, heat gun, hammer,
mallet, pry bar, clamp, or power tool is permitted.

Confirm all of the following before assembly:

1. Every part ID, slicer job, PETG spool lot, drying record, scale, orientation,
   and photo ID is traceable.
2. Every part cooled before removal and passed the inspection table in
   `PRINT_FIRST.md`.
3. Only the outer brim was removed. No dovetail, channel, shoulder, keyway,
   retainer, support land, or printed bore was sanded, filed, reamed, drilled,
   heated, or forced to fit.
4. There is no crack, layer split, warp, under-extrusion, contamination,
   whitening, missing feature, slicer repair, or loose fragment.
5. The three logs and matching retainers remain labeled rear, center, and
   front. The two cassette hands and two support-retainer hands remain labeled.

If a part fails any check, quarantine it and record the failure. Do not rescue
the test by modifying the interface.

## Learn the directions

- **Rear** is the wall side of the eventual shelf; **front** is the room side.
- **Left** and **right** are the authored file hands, viewed from the room.
- **Toward the bay** means inward between the two supports: rightward at the
  left support and leftward at the right support.
- **Away from the bay** is the reverse: leftward at the left support and
  rightward at the right support.

The support retainers are bayonet bars. Each has a narrow shaft, a rear dog,
and a front handle. They insert from the shelf front, rear-dog first, then move
sideways 2.4 mm. They are retention-only parts; the cassette must bear directly
on the broad support capitals above them.

## Core one-bay assembly — exact authored order

Work dry and off the supports for Steps 1–3. Stop at the first resistance; a
correct motion is controlled and hand-operated, never hammered or twisted.

1. off the supports, slide the three independent dovetail logs into the left cassette half
2. with the right half absent, lower each independent retainer through the left-half top access into its exposed log notch
3. slide the right half over the logs and retainer ends so its closed top captures all three keys
4. place two printed supports at the 254 mm planning centers
5. lower the joined cassette onto its two 15.70 mm physical support contacts
6. at each cassette/support contact, insert its bay-local support retainer straight from the front and shift it 2.4 mm toward that bay to place its rear dog behind the positive shoulder; the integrated handle remains 4.0 mm proud for a hand grip
7. for disassembly shift each support retainer 2.4 mm away from its bay, grasp the 4.0 mm handle and pull it straight forward, lift the cassette clear of both lugs, then reverse the off-support slide

For a novice, Steps 1–7 mean:

1. Place the left cassette half on the padded table. Slide the rear, center, and
   front logs linearly into their matching channels until each positive body
   shoulder seats. Do not use a retainer to pull a log home.
2. Leave the right half off. Lower the matching independent retainer through
   each top access and into the exposed notch of its one log. The retainer's
   integrated cap must close that same access flush; there are no separate
   debris-cover pieces.
3. Keep all three keys seated. Align the right cassette half with the three
   exposed log/key ends, then slide it straight onto them. Its closed top traps
   the keys. Do not rock, twist, or clamp the halves together.
4. Set the supports upright on the table with their centers 254 mm apart and
   their broad capital lands level. This is a tabletop arrangement only; do
   not put screws through the three printed candidate bores.
5. Lower the joined cassette vertically until both ends have complete, direct
   contact on the 15.70 mm support half-lands. A retainer cannot compensate for
   a gap, rock, or partial bearing.
6. At the left support, insert the left support retainer straight from the
   front, rear-dog first, then shift it exactly 2.4 mm rightward into the bay.
   At the right support, insert the right retainer from the front and shift it
   exactly 2.4 mm leftward into the bay. Each rear dog must finish behind its
   positive shoulder while the front handle remains serviceable.
7. Verify that all three midpoint caps are flush, all positive log shoulders
   are seated, the 0.35 mm physical midpoint seam is not forced closed, both
   support retainers are in their shifted positions, both support lands bear
   fully, and the assembly is square and flat.

Do not place books, tools, test weights, or body weight on the bay. The only
permitted action here is gentle, unloaded fit cycling.

## Unloaded ten-cycle check

One cycle is a complete assembly and complete disassembly by the exact reverse
path. Do ten cycles and record every one in `PRINT_FIRST.md`.

To disassemble:

1. Shift the left support retainer 2.4 mm leftward, away from the bay, and pull
   it straight forward. Shift the right retainer 2.4 mm rightward and pull it
   straight forward.
2. Lift the joined cassette straight up until it clears both support lugs.
3. Move the supports aside. Slide the right cassette half straight off the
   three captured log/key ends.
4. Lift each log retainer vertically out through its left-half top access.
5. Slide the three logs straight out of the left half.
6. Inspect all shoulders, notches, caps, channels, dogs, handles, keyways,
   support lugs, and bearing lands before starting the next cycle.

Stop and fail the gate for force, cracking, whitening, shaving, growing dust,
progressive looseness, rocking, a proud cap, incomplete shoulder seating,
uncommanded key movement, a support retainer that bypasses its positive stop,
or damage that increases with cycling.

## Separate S0 cable-bookend service check

The fused two-socket receiver belongs only on first-wall support S0, the
far-left outer bookend. Both sockets face inward. Intermediate supports and the
inside-corner placeholder receive no cable receiver or peg.

Test on the padded table, separate from the structural one-bay assembly:

1. Inspect the additive receiver and its print ramp. Any receiver/root crack or
   separation fails the article.
2. Hold one flush blank at socket 0's upper entry. Move it straight inward
   through the 6 mm approach, then lower it exactly 8 mm into its gravity seat.
   Lift 8 mm and move straight outward to remove it. Never bend or snap it in.
3. Repeat ten cycles in socket 0 and ten in socket 1, one occupied socket at a
   time. Then repeat with the comb/hook, again one socket at a time.
4. After single-socket cycling passes, install a flush blank in every unused
   socket. The normal furniture state is two blanks, or one cable comb/hook and
   one blank.
5. Use representative loose cables only for later snag/handling observation.
   Never hang shelf-test mass, tools, or body weight from the comb/hook.

Passing this tabletop service path does not resolve the still-unmeasured door,
trim, outlet, plug, cord-loop, snag, or human-access envelopes.
