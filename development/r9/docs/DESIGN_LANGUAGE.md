# R9 Palatine Moderne design language

## The selected character

The first shelf uses **Palatine Moderne**: a restrained blend of Roman civic
architecture and 1920s–1930s Art Deco. It should read as intentional built-in
furniture, not a novelty prop and not a row of oversized columns.

The hierarchy is simple:

1. a black horizontal shelf plane;
2. repeated compressed Roman arch/corbel silhouettes below it;
3. a stepped keystone centered in each visible structural arch;
4. a shallow stepped center relief on each front-beam bay; and
5. stronger detailing only at the far-left bookend and, later, the far-right
   bookend on the return wall.

## Exact rules

- Structural straps, fastener lands, sockets, cassette locators, and member
  interfaces stay plain and dimensionally controlled.
- Ornament is additive-only in the current one-bay prototype. It may not cut a
  load web, thin a root, cross a screw/washer land, or reduce an interface.
- The support keystone is full thickness through the 32 mm support body, so the
  broad-face print remains one connected repeating profile.
- The front-beam relief starts on the build plate and grows continuously; it
  does not create a floating decorative ledge.
- Steps use crisp 4 mm vertical increments on the support and shallow 1 mm
  fascia projections. Tiny faux carving, fragile tips, and glued appliques are
  excluded.
- Matte/semi-gloss black PETG provides the shadow lines. Gold paint, imitation
  marble, lettering, and high-contrast trim are not part of the base design.
- The ordinary middle supports remain short. Visual emphasis belongs at the
  endpoints and at the repeated keystone rhythm, not in new floor-to-shelf
  columns.

## What v3 actually emits

The Palatine Moderne one-bay v3 geometry emits:

- a stepped Roman keystone inside each handed compact support arch;
- a two-step vertical center relief on the front beam;
- the unchanged structural wall strap, three-hole washer land, member sockets,
  locator bosses, rear ledger, and three-web cassette; and
- no cosmetic subtraction from a structural body.

The complete long-wall entablature, far-left feature bookend, corner transition,
and seam-cover rhythm are end-product work. They must be generated from the
measured shelf segmentation after the one-bay and hardware fixtures pass.

## Visual acceptance

At tabletop review, look from six feet away before inspecting layer lines. The
silhouette should read first, the arch/keystone second, and the print texture
third. Reject a decorative revision if it makes the middle supports feel like
columns, creates dust-catching pockets, obstructs storage, or disguises access
to a wall screw.
